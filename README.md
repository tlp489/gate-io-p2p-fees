## What Gate P2P actually is

Gate's P2P product is a marketplace, not an order book. Sellers post ads with a price, a payment method, and a trade limit. Buyers browse those ads, pick one, and pay the seller directly through a bank transfer or mobile wallet. Gate never touches the fiat.

What Gate does handle is the crypto side. When you place an order, the seller's coins are locked in escrow immediately. They can't be moved, spent, or withdrawn. The coins only release to you after the seller confirms your payment landed in their account. Gate also runs the dispute process if something goes wrong.

That escrow step is the whole reason this is safer than messaging a stranger on Telegram. You're not trusting the counterparty — you're trusting a lock that neither side can open early.

Gate lists **USDT, BTC, USDC, ETH and DOGE** as the supported assets on the P2P side, with coverage across **450+ payment channels in roughly 80 countries**, according to Gate's own P2P guide. Alipay and WeChat appear among the supported methods in Gate's help documentation, and regional rails like bKash and Nagad show up in Gate's community posts for Bangladesh. Availability is country-specific, so the honest answer for your region is the one you get after logging in and opening the P2P market.

The other number that matters: **0%**. Gate charges no platform trading fee on P2P orders, for both buyers and sellers. That's not a promo — it's the standing policy, and it's the main reason P2P tends to beat card purchases on cost.

## Before your first trade: three things that must be done

You can register an account without verification, but you can't deposit, withdraw, or use the P2P market. KYC is the gate.

**Identity verification.** Gate's KYC accepts a passport or national ID, plus facial recognition. It's a one-time process. Approval time varies by region and document quality — some users clear it in minutes, others wait a business day or two. Blurry photos, mismatched names, and unsupported document types are the usual rejection reasons.

**Two-factor authentication.** P2P requires 2FA on the account. Set it up before you try to trade, not during.

**At least one payment method in your P2P profile.** You register the account you'll pay from. This matters more than people expect.

That last point connects to the rule that causes the most failed trades: **the legal name on your payment account must match the verified name on your Gate account exactly.** Not similar. Not "my company account." The same name. If your bank account is in your mother's name, that trade will not work, and in the worst case you'll have sent money to a seller who can't legally release the escrow to you.

## The full buy flow, step by step

The web and app flows are close enough that one description covers both.

1. **Go to the P2P market.** On the web you'll find it under Buy Crypto → P2P Trading. In the app it's Assets → Overview → Deposit → P2P.
2. **Pick your coin and your fiat currency.** USDT is the default for most people — it's the pairing that has the deepest pool of sellers and the tightest pricing.
3. **Filter the ad list.** You can sort by payment method, price, and trade size. Two filters do most of the work: payment method and seller reputation.
4. **Check completion rate before price.** A seller at a slightly worse rate with a 98% completion rate is usually the better trade than a marginally cheaper ad from someone with a thin history. Gate's merchant badges — Blue V and Crown — are assigned based on trading volume, completion rate, and compliance record, so they're a reasonable shortcut. Verified merchant plus high completion rate is the combination to look for.
5. **Enter the amount and place the order.** The ad's own limits govern how much you can trade in a single order.
6. **Pay the seller, then tap "I have paid."** You get **20 minutes** from order placement. Gate does not support automatic payments — the confirmation is on you. Miss the window and the order auto-cancels and the escrow releases back to the seller. If you've already sent the money at that point, you're in a dispute, not a trade.
7. **Wait for the release.** After you mark payment, the seller checks their bank or wallet and confirms. Gate's own documentation puts the typical arrival window at **15–30 minutes** after you click "I have paid." Both sides are rated afterward, which is how the reputation system gets its data.

Total realistic time end to end: five to thirty minutes, depending mostly on how fast the seller checks their account.

## The fee question: 0% is real, but it isn't the whole cost

This is where most guides get lazy. "Zero fees" is true and also incomplete.

**What you don't pay:** Gate's platform commission. Nothing. It's 0% for buyers and sellers at every volume level, and unlike some competitors, Gate hasn't introduced a maker fee for P2P merchants. That's a real difference — Bybit and Bitget both moved to charging P2P merchant maker fees, which makes Gate's zero-fee stance a genuine outlier among the major exchanges.

**What you do pay:** The seller's margin, baked into the quoted rate. Sellers price their ads above the spot market — typically somewhere in the **0.5%–1%** range, though this moves with local demand, liquidity, and how competitive your currency's market happens to be. You can see it yourself by comparing the P2P rate on the ad against the spot price of the same coin. That spread is the seller's revenue.

**What your payment rail might charge:** Domestic bank transfers are often free. Digital wallets and cross-border methods sometimes carry their own fee or spread. That cost belongs to your bank or wallet provider, not Gate.

So the honest framing: a $1,000 P2P purchase typically costs you a few dollars in spread, versus a card purchase where Gate's own comparison guides put the platform fee at **up to around 3.5%** — meaning $35-plus on the same $1,000, before any bank-side charges. ## Limits: who sets what

There are two different limit systems in play, and conflating them causes confusion.

**Per-order limits are set by the seller.** Each ad specifies a minimum and maximum trade size in your local currency. If you want to move a large amount, you're looking for a seller whose ad window covers it — and merchants can post ads up to **$100,000** once they reach Blue V status.

**Account-level limits come from your verification level and region.** Higher KYC tiers widen what you can trade, and some regions carry regulatory caps that don't exist elsewhere.

Practically: if you're trying to buy more than the ads you're seeing will allow, the fix is usually to look at verified merchants rather than to split the order across several small sellers.

## Selling on Gate P2P

The flow reverses with one important difference in risk. As a seller, you post an ad, your crypto gets locked in escrow when a buyer orders, and you release only after verifying the money actually arrived in your account.

Do not release on a screenshot. Screenshots are trivially forged, and "the payment is pending on my end" is the oldest line in the P2P playbook. Check your actual bank or wallet balance. If the funds aren't there, it isn't a completed payment, no matter what the chat says.

If the buyer becomes unresponsive, you file a dispute. Gate's support team reviews the internal chat log, payment evidence, and transaction IDs, then decides whether to release the escrow to the buyer or return it to you. Support runs 24/7.

## Where P2P goes wrong, and how to avoid it

Almost every bad P2P story traces back to the same handful of mistakes.

**Paying outside the platform.** If a seller asks you to take the conversation to WhatsApp or Telegram and settle there, you've lost escrow protection entirely. Every message belongs in the platform chat — it's also the record Gate uses if there's a dispute.

**Releasing before the money lands.** Sellers, this is on you. Confirm the balance, not the notification.

**Ignoring the 20-minute window.** The clock starts when you place the order, not when you finish your bank's verification process. If your bank is slow, some sellers offer longer windows — check the ad terms before ordering.

**Mismatched payment names.** Covered above, but it's the single most common cause of stuck orders.

**Chasing the cheapest ad.** The bottom of the price list is where the low-completion-rate sellers live. A fraction of a percent isn't worth a dispute.

Gate has also been tightening the community side of this. A **malicious conduct reporting reward program** went live in **March 2026**, letting users report protocol violations for rewards.
